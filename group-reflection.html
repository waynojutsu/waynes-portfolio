Teamwork through Tuckman's five stages

I led our CSS group presentation on first-year student success at Botswana School of Business Sciences. We had to turn a broad topic into something other first-year students could follow. Tuckman’s five stages help me look back at how we divided up the work and brought it together.

Forming

We started by working out what the brief asked for and where each person could contribute. First-year student success is a broad topic. We needed a common idea of what to include and how much detail would fit in a presentation.

Storming

The main challenge was deciding what to keep. Some ideas overlapped, so we had to work out what added something and what repeated another person’s point. I learned to bring the conversation back to the brief and hear people’s reasons before making a decision.

Norming

As the structure took shape, our responsibilities became clearer. The presentation covered study habits, settling into college and student support. I worked on how to introduce those points and how to bring them together in the conclusion.

Performing

Once we had the individual sections, the next job was making them flow as one presentation. A useful slide could still feel out of place if it came too early or repeated a point. Reviewing the transitions helped us keep the explanations easier to follow.

Adjourning

After the presentation, I looked back at how we had organised the work. Next time I would agree on responsibilities and a review date earlier so we have time to check the full presentation together. Leading this group taught me to pay as much attention to listening and coordinating as I do to presenting.
